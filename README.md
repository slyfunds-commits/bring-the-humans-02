## What is make.fun?

make.fun is a place where people and their AI coding agents build software for bounties. Each bounty has its own task and rules; eligible entries can earn USDC rewards on Solana. You bring the idea and judgment, and an agent such as Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, or Copilot can help build the software.

A reward is never automatic just because you entered. Read the bounty's rules before you start: they explain what to make, how entries are checked or scored, when entries close, and how any reward is paid.

## How a bounty works

1. **Choose:** pick a bounty accepting entries and read the complete task, requirements, deadline, and payout details.
2. **Prepare:** get its kit and follow its instructions. The kit may include rules, tools, or a starter for that specific task.
3. **Build:** work with your coding agent and check progress against the deliverables, not just whether the project runs.
4. **Submit:** use the required format. A GitHub entry can require a public repository at a particular commit, and some bounties require agent-session transcripts.
5. **Follow up:** check your entry's status. If a check fails, use its feedback and resubmit only while the bounty's rules permit.
6. **Claim:** if your entry places and passes the payout requirements, follow the claim instructions for that bounty.

The details vary by bounty. The page and kit are the source of truth; don't assume that one bounty's submission format or scoring process applies to another.

## What you'll need

- **A Solana wallet** for receiving a reward. Keep control of your wallet and its recovery information.
- **A GitHub account** if the bounty asks for a GitHub repository. Check whether it must be public and whether your make.fun account needs that GitHub account linked.
- **An AI coding agent**, for example Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, or Copilot. Use a tool that can work with the files and session records the bounty requires.
- **Time to read and review.** You decide what to build and whether the finished entry actually follows the rules.

## 2. Pick a bounty

Browse the bounty board and open a task that interests you. Before committing to it, look for:

- The exact deliverable: what you need to build and what files or links to submit.
- Any prerequisites, tools, or accounts the bounty requires.
- How entries are checked and scored, and any tie-breaking rules.
- The entry deadline, reward amounts, and payout instructions.

If something is unclear, use the bounty's rules and requirements rather than guessing. Choose a task you can finish and verify before its deadline.

## 3. Get the kit

On the bounty page, open the kit and follow its setup instructions. Start by reading `RULES.txt`, then check whether the kit has a starter, command-line tool, or local check. Use only what the bounty describes; not every bounty has the same kit or a local grader.

Keep the kit and your entry in the locations its instructions expect. If a local check is provided, run it as directed before submission. A local check can help catch mistakes, but the bounty's own checks and decision are what matter.

## 4. Build your entry

Start with a small plan: translate the task into the deliverables and checks listed in the rules. Ask your coding agent to inspect the kit and requirements before it changes files. For example:

> Read this bounty's `RULES.txt` and requirements first. Summarize what the entry must contain, how it is checked, and the deadline. Then help me build an entry that meets those rules. Don't submit anything until I have reviewed it.

Build in manageable steps and review the result yourself. If the task is too large for the time you have, reduce the scope without dropping a required deliverable.

If the bounty requires a GitHub repository, create or use one owned by the GitHub account linked to your make.fun account. Make it public if required, add the license the rules ask for, and keep the requested entry files at the expected paths. If transcripts are required, build in a coding-agent environment that records the sessions and follow the kit's instructions for bundling them.

Before submitting, compare the entry with the rules line by line. Check that the README, links, filenames, license, and any required screenshots are present and that the repository contains no files the bounty forbids.

## 5. Submit it

Submit through the bounty's entry form or the method its kit specifies. Before you send it, check this short list:

- The repository belongs to the GitHub account linked to your make.fun account, if the bounty requires that.
- The repository URL and commit are the ones you intend to enter.
- Every required file and any required session transcripts are included.
- You are still within the entry deadline.

Don't wait until the last minute: leave time to upload, read the checks, and correct a fixable problem before entries close. A submission is not complete just because your code is pushed to GitHub.

## 6. Check the result

After submitting, return to the bounty or your profile and look at the entry's status. Read each check and its reason rather than guessing what went wrong. If something fails, use the stated fix, then resubmit only if the bounty is still accepting entries and its rules allow it.

Once scoring or judging is finished, check the final result on the bounty page. A provisional status or early count is not necessarily the final outcome.

## 7. Claim your reward

If your entry wins a paid place, follow the claim instructions shown for that bounty and your account. Rewards are paid in USDC on Solana; the bounty's payout details tell you whether a wallet signature, payout information, or another step is needed. Review the destination carefully before confirming. Never share your wallet's recovery phrase or private key.

## Troubleshooting: common mistakes

- **Treating a successful kit check as a submission.** A local or pre-submit check can catch problems, but it does not by itself enter your work. Follow the bounty's separate submission steps, then confirm that your entry appears in its status.
- **Starting from the wrong referral link.** Open your intended referral link before creating your account, or enter its code during sign-up if the site asks. Referral attribution is based on the first link the browser saw for 60 days, so don't assume adding a code later will replace an earlier referral.
- **Leaving a required repository private.** If the bounty asks for a public GitHub repository, check its visibility before submitting; a private repository cannot meet that requirement.
- **Putting the README in the wrong place.** When a bounty expects its guide at the repository root, an existing `.github/README.md` can be shown in place of `README.md`. Remove the shadowing file so visitors and checks see the intended guide.
- **Submitting the wrong account's repository.** For a bounty that requires a repository owned by your linked GitHub account, verify both the owner and the account linked to make.fun before you submit.
- **Waiting until after entries close to fix a problem.** A check can identify a fix, but a corrected submission still has to meet the bounty's deadline and resubmission rules. Leave time to act on feedback.

## Quick FAQ

**Do I need to know how to code?**  
You can ask an AI coding agent to help build the entry, but you still choose the task, review what it makes, and check that it follows the rules.

**Does passing the kit check mean I've entered?**  
No. Use the bounty's submission method as well, and check the entry status afterward to confirm it was received.

**Do all bounties accept the same kind of entry?**  
No. Requirements differ. One may ask for a repository and commit, while another may specify a different format or extra materials. Read that bounty's task and kit.

**When do I get paid?**  
Only entries that place and meet the payout requirements can claim a reward. Check the bounty and your entry status for the result and the claim steps; payout timing and any additional requirements depend on the bounty.
